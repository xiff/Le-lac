Neoprene and winter swimming: wetsuit, gloves and boots

By the My Lake team · published 15 November 2026

In short

A neoprene wetsuit helps you retain warmth and float, especially below 18 °C, but it prevents neither cold shock nor hypothermia, and RLSS advises beginners against water below 12 °C even in a wetsuit. For classic winter swimming, Helsana lists a hat, a tow float, a towel and warm clothes, and, if needed, neoprene gloves and socks.

What a wetsuit does, and what it doesn’t

According to RLSS, swimming in a wetsuit in open water is generally safer, especially when the water is 18 °C or lower: it helps retain warmth and improves buoyancy. But you can still experience cold shock and hypothermia in a wetsuit, and RLSS does not recommend that beginners swim in water below 12 °C. A wetsuit therefore does not replace gradual acclimatisation.

Which thickness?

RLSS gives a guide, to be taken as a rough order of magnitude:

  • above 20 °C: not necessarily a wetsuit, a thin 1 to 2 mm one may be enough;
  • 16 to 20 °C: 2 or 3 mm;
  • 14 to 17 °C: 3 or 4 mm;
  • 11 to 14 °C: 4 or 5 mm;
  • below 12 °C: 5 mm or more.

The choice also depends on how much you feel the cold, your ability and the conditions. A well-fitting wetsuit keeps you warm because it limits the water circulating between suit and skin, without restricting movement. Try several models.

Gloves, boots and the rest

For winter swimming without a wetsuit, Helsana recommends a hat (swim cap or wool), a tow float or dry bag, a towel, warm clothes and a hot drink for afterwards, and, if needed, neoprene gloves and socks. And, for safety, to come with a second person. The full list: kit for winter swimming.

After the swim

Wet neoprene chills: get out of it quickly and dress in dry clothes. The Swiss Lifesaving Society advises against a hot shower right after the swim and recommends a lukewarm drink: more on afterdrop.

Disclaimer. This article gives general information taken from the sources cited; it is not medical advice. Cold-water swimming puts real strain on the heart and can be dangerous: talk to a doctor before you start, especially with a heart condition, high blood pressure or during pregnancy. Never swim alone and follow lifeguards’ instructions. You swim at your own risk; in an emergency, call 144 in Switzerland or 112.
