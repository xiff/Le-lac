The level of Lake Geneva: a lake regulated between 371.7 and 372.3 m

By the My Lake team · published 18 November 2026

In short

The level of Lake Geneva has been artificially regulated between 371.7 and 372.3 metres above sea level since 1884. Before the dam, Forel measured an average difference of 1.54 m between summer and winter, because of the Rhône. The mean level measured between 1943 and 2008 is 372.05 m.

Who sets the level?

The level has been artificially regulated since 1884, by a movable-curtain dam at the Pont de la Machine in Geneva, following an inter-cantonal convention of 1884 and a federal regulation of 1892. The Rhône upstream controls the lake’s hydrography: its mean flow was 179.2 m³/s between 2005 and 2015, and its variations, mostly due to glaciers, made the level fluctuate by an average of 1.54 m between summer and winter, from François-Alphonse Forel’s measurements before the dam.

The levels

  • Regulation: between 371.7 and 372.3 m.
  • Average between 1943 and 2008: 372.05 m; minimum of 371.78 m in 1949, maximum of 372.19 m in 1977.
  • 1984 agreement: 371.6 m from March to April and 372.3 m from June to December.
  • Every leap year the level is lowered to 371.45 m to allow maintenance work along the shores.

Depth

The maximum depth is 309.7 m and the mean depth 152.7 m. On level changes of a few centimetres within an hour: seiches.
