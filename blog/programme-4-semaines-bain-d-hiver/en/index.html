Starting winter swimming: a 4-week programme

By the My Lake team · published 15 November 2026

In short

This is our suggestion in four stages, built on the rules in the sources: start with a few seconds (Helsana), get in slowly, never exceed as many minutes as the water has degrees (Swiss Lifesaving Society), go with someone. It is a frame of caution, not a medical protocol or a promise of results: anyone with a health concern talks to their doctor first.

The rules that set the frame

  • Time ceiling: as many minutes as the water has degrees (5 minutes at 5 °C), and it is a maximum, not a target (Swiss Lifesaving Society, Helsana).
  • Start small: at first a few seconds are enough; build up over time (Helsana).
  • Get in slowly, wet your body gradually, avoid putting your head under (Helsana).
  • Never alone: a second person comes with you (Helsana).
  • Start in summer: regular outdoor swims before autumn get the body used to lower temperatures (Helsana).
  • No beginners below 12 °C, wetsuit or not: a wetsuit prevents neither cold shock nor hypothermia (RLSS, UK).

Week 1: get used to the water

Two or three outings. Go in up to your knees, then your waist, for a few seconds, without ducking. Get out before you are really cold. The aim: for the gasp reflex (see the article) to be less of a surprise.

Week 2: up to the shoulders

Go in slowly up to your shoulders, head out of the water, for a few tens of seconds up to a minute. Stay where you can stand, and wait for your breathing to settle before moving your arms.

Week 3: a few minutes, carefully

Move to one or two minutes, then three if the water allows. Check the current temperature before you get in: the ceiling of the rule drops with the water. For women, Helsana reports that experts recommend gentler variants, one to three minutes in water at 10 to 12 °C, especially in the second half of the cycle.

Week 4: settle your routine

Stay under the ceiling of the rule, and preferably well under it. Pick a beach where you can get out easily and change under shelter, come with the same person, and plan the warm-up and the kit before you get in.

When to stop or to stay out

Get out as soon as you feel uncomfortable. Graubünden Ferien discourages icy-water bathing for high blood pressure, diabetes, and heart or vascular disease; Helsana advises giving it up or asking your doctor if you have a health problem or any doubt. Disclaimer. This article gives general information taken from the sources cited; it is not medical advice. Cold-water swimming puts real strain on the heart and can be dangerous: talk to a doctor before you start, especially with a heart condition, high blood pressure or during pregnancy. Never swim alone and follow lifeguards’ instructions. You swim at your own risk; in an emergency, call 144 in Switzerland or 112.
