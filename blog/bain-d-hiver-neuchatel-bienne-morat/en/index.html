Winter swimming in the Three Lakes: where to swim and warm up

By the My Lake team · published 8 November 2026

In short

For a winter swim in Lake Neuchâtel, Lake Biel or Lake Murten, the Swiss Lifesaving Society gives a simple rule: no more minutes in the water than it has degrees. My Lake shows today’s water in front of each beach on the three lakes, and lists 4 lakeside saunas for warming up.

How long should you stay in?

The Swiss Lifesaving Society gives a simple rule for cold water: do not stay in for more minutes than the water has degrees. The current water temperature is shown below. Read first: the first 90 seconds in cold water and how to warm up afterwards.

Warming up: the lakeside saunas

Lake Neuchâtel

  • Les Rives Zen, Estavayer-le-Lac, near Estavayer – Nouvelle Plage: Wood-fired sauna and cold dip on Lake Neuchâtel: CHF 25 for 2 hours, CHF 35 for 3 hours, up to 16 people.
  • Les P’tits Baigneurs, Boudry, near Boudry: Wood-fired barrel sauna on Boudry beach, up to 6 people, by booking, in winter.

Lake Biel

  • Saunafloss Erlach, Cerlier (Erlach), near Cerlier (Erlach): Wood-fired sauna rafts moored at the Erlach landing stage on Lake Biel: straight into the lake afterwards. By booking.
  • Saunaboot Biel, Bienne (Vingelz), near Bienne – Plage: Sauna boat on Lake Biel, run by Joran-Biel (Neuenburgstrasse 72, bus stop Räblus Vingelz).

Entries checked on 11 November 2026. Opening hours, prices and seasons change: each sauna’s link goes to its own website, worth checking before you set off.

Disclaimer. This article gives general information taken from the sources cited; it is not medical advice. Cold-water swimming puts real strain on the heart and can be dangerous: talk to a doctor before you start, especially with a heart condition, high blood pressure or during pregnancy. Never swim alone and follow lifeguards’ instructions. You swim at your own risk; in an emergency, call 144 in Switzerland or 112.
