Lake turnover: oxygen for the depths

By the My Lake team · published 20 November 2026

In short

Turnover is the vertical mixing of a lake’s water at the end of the year. A complete turnover needs very cold winters, when surface water brings its oxygen to the depths: they are becoming rarer. In winter 2017, Lake Geneva was mixed down to 200 m, but not completely: the lake is over 300 m deep.

The layers of the lake

In warm weather, lakes form layers of distinct temperatures: the epilimnion on top, the thermocline in the middle, the colder hypolimnion down to the bottom. See lake stratification.

What happens at the end of the year?

Wikipedia mentions vertical currents, the “turnover of the lake at the end of the year”. When surface water cools, it can sink and mix the water column.

Why cold winters matter

According to Wikipedia’s “Léman” page, complete turnovers need very cold winters, during which surface water brings its oxygen to the depths; they are becoming rarer, which, along with climate warming, points to new adaptations of the ecosystem. In winter 2017 the waters were mixed down to 200 m; according to CIPEL, this turnover was not complete, the lake being more than 300 m deep.

For swimming

Turnover does not change what you feel at the beach day to day. It happens in winter and at depth; it is followed for the lake’s health. On surface temperature: Lake Geneva is warming.
