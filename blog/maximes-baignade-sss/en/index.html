The six swimming safety rules, explained

By the My Lake team · published 23 November 2026

In short

The Swiss Lifesaving Society (SLRG) sums up swimming in six rules: 1) children at the water’s edge are always accompanied; 2) no alcohol, no full or empty stomach; 3) no jumping in after prolonged sunbathing; 4) no diving or jumping into murky or unknown water; 5) no air mattresses in deep water; 6) never swim long distances alone.

The six rules

  • 1. Children at the water’s edge must always be accompanied; small children are kept within arm’s reach.
  • 2. Never swim after alcohol or drugs, or on a full or completely empty stomach.
  • 3. Never jump into the water after prolonged sunbathing: the body needs time to adapt.
  • 4. Do not dive or jump into murky or unknown water.
  • 5. Air mattresses and any swimming aids must not be used in deep water.
  • 6. Never swim long distances alone.

Why these six?

The SLRG explains each rule on its page: small children can drown in under 20 seconds, silently, even in shallow water; alcohol impairs awareness and makes people overestimate themselves; the thermal shock after sunbathing can cause cramps, faintness, even fainting; unseen dangers hide in unknown water; a mattress can lose its air; even the best-trained body can fail.

We cover each in an article: children, eating, drinking and sunbathing, diving into the unknown, mattresses and floats, swimming alone.

Disclaimer. This article gives general information taken from the sources cited; it is not medical advice. Cold-water swimming puts real strain on the heart and can be dangerous: talk to a doctor before you start, especially with a heart condition, high blood pressure or during pregnancy. Never swim alone and follow lifeguards’ instructions. You swim at your own risk; in an emergency, call 144 in Switzerland or 112.
